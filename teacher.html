👩‍🏫 Teacher's plan

🔤 ABC & 123 →

For two 7-year-old beginners who can't read yet, taught one-to-one with Family and Friends 1 (2nd edition). Two lessons a week, 45–60 minutes each.

The idea in one line: follow the book in order, take each unit slower than a school class would, and add 10 minutes of letter sounds to every lesson. The book teaches one letter sound for each letter of the alphabet in Units 1–6, so by Unit 6 they know all 26.
  • Talk first, read later. Most of each lesson is listening, pointing, singing and acting. Writing is tracing and copying only.
  • Sounds before names. Teach "b" as the sound in bird, not "bee". Letter names come in Unit 7.
  • About 3 weeks per unit (6 lessons). Slow down whenever a child needs it. The weeks are a guide.
  • Every lesson: hello song → review → new words → book page → letter corner → game → goodbye. Details in the Lessons tab.

Every 4–5 weeks, a 5-minute check. Show each letter learned so far: can they say its sound? Ask 5 questions from the units (What's this? How old are you? What colour is it?). Note what they know. If a child is slow on letters, stay a week longer before the next unit's letters.

Each lesson is about 50 minutes. For 45 minutes, shorten the game. For 60, add a workbook page or a craft. Track numbers are the Class Book audio (the 🔊 numbers next to each exercise in the book).

Lesson 1 · Hello! Starter, pages 4–5

By the end they can: say Hello and Goodbye, and answer What's your name? with My name's… They know the sound of a.

You need: Class Book pages 4–5, audio tracks 01–04, a puppet or soft toy, an apple (or a picture of one), paper and crayons, a phone or laptop with the Tap a letter page.
  1. 5 min Hello. Wave: "Hello! My name's Anna." The puppet says hello too. Wave until the child waves and says "Hello" back. One sentence in their language is fine to explain that the lesson is in English.
  2. 8 min Meet the characters (p. 4, ex. 1, track 01). Listen and point to Rosy, Tim, Billy and Miss Jones. Then play "Where's Billy?": you say a name, they point.
  3. 5 min Chant (ex. 2, track 02). Clap along. Second time, they wave on every "Hello".
  4. 10 min Story (ex. 3, track 03). Listen and point to each picture. Then act it out: the puppet asks "What's your name?" and the child answers "My name's ___." Swap: the child asks the puppet.
  5. 7 min Song (p. 5, ex. 3, track 04). The child sings their own name in the gap. Wave on "Goodbye".
  6. 10 min Letter corner: a. Open Tap a letter, press Aa and listen. Show the apple: "a, a, apple." The child presses Aa three times and copies the sound. Draw a big a in the air, on their palm with a finger, then with a crayon on paper.
  7. 3 min Goodbye. A sticker, then "Goodbye, ___!" with a wave.

Homework: say "Hello, my name's ___" to someone at home. Press Aa on the Tap a letter page. Workbook: the Starter page.

✅ Check: answers "What's your name?" without help.

Lesson 2 · Numbers and age Starter, pages 5–6

By the end they can: count 1–10, ask What's your name? and answer How old are you? with I'm seven. They know b.

You need: tracks 04–06, 10 small things to count (blocks, buttons), number cards 1–10, a dice, the puppet.
  1. 4 min Hello song (track 04).
  2. 5 min Review (p. 5, ex. 2). This time the child asks the puppet "What's your name?" and the puppet answers.
  3. 10 min Numbers 1–10 (p. 6, ex. 1, track 05). Listen, point, repeat. Count fingers, then blocks. Put number cards on the floor: "Jump to four!"
  4. 6 min Listen and tick (ex. 2, track 06). Do the first one together.
  5. 7 min How old are you? (ex. 3). Point to children in the picture and ask. Then ask the child: "I'm seven," showing seven fingers. Ask about the puppet, their brother or sister. A number alone is a fine answer.
  6. 5 min Dice game. Roll, count the dots out loud, move a toy that many steps.
  7. 10 min Letter corner: b. Review a on Tap a letter. New: b as in bird. Clap game: you say a word, they clap if it starts with "b" (ball, bag, banana… cat, dog, apple).
  8. 3 min Goodbye.

Homework: count 1–10 at home (stairs, toys). On the ABC & 123 page: Learn numbers 1–10, and press Aa and Bb.

✅ Check: counts to 10, says "I'm seven."

Lesson 3 · Days and colours Starter, pages 6–7

By the end they can: name the 7 colours on page 7 and say what day it is today. They know c.

You need: tracks 07–08, crayons or paper in red, yellow, pink, green, purple, orange, blue, a blank sheet for a rainbow.
  1. 3 min Hello song.
  2. 5 min Review. Flash number cards fast. Ask "How old are you?"
  3. 6 min Days chant (p. 6, ex. 4, track 07). Listen and point to each day. Don't expect them to learn all seven. Today's day is enough: "Today is Monday."
  4. 10 min Colours (p. 7, ex. 1, track 08). Listen, point, repeat. Then "Touch something red!" around the room.
  5. 8 min Song (ex. 2–3). Listen and point to each colour on the rainbow. Second time, hold up the matching crayon.
  6. 5 min Match (ex. 4). You read the colour words; the child says the colour and draws the line with you.
  7. 10 min Letter corner: c. Review a b. New: c as in cat. Draw a big c in their favourite colour, or make it from play-dough.
  8. 3 min Goodbye.

Homework: colour a rainbow and say the colours to someone at home. Press Aa, Bb, Cc on Tap a letter.

✅ Check: names at least 5 colours when you point.

Lesson 4 · What's this? Unit 1, pages 8–9

By the end they can: name pen, pencil, rubber, ruler, book and answer What's this? with It's a pen. They know d, so all four sounds of Unit 1 are ready before its phonics page.

You need: a real pen, pencil, rubber, ruler and book in a bag, tracks 10–12, a sheet of paper to cover pictures.
  1. 3 min Hello song.
  2. 5 min Review colours. Hold up a crayon: "What colour is it?" (they hear the question form they need today).
  3. 10 min New words (p. 8, ex. 1, track 10). Take each thing out of the bag. Then "Mystery bag": the child feels one thing inside the bag and guesses.
  4. 4 min Chant (ex. 2, track 11).
  5. 8 min Story (ex. 3, track 12). Listen and point. Act it out with the real things.
  6. 8 min What's this? It's a… (p. 9, ex. 2). Cover a picture and slide the paper away slowly. You ask "What's this?", they answer "It's a pencil." Then swap. Skip the writing in ex. 3 for now, or write it yourself and let them trace "It's".
  7. 10 min Letter corner: d. Review a b c. New: d as in dog. Sound bingo: you say a sound, they press the right letter on Tap a letter.
  8. 2 min Goodbye.

Homework: find 5 school things at home and say them in English. Press Aa to Dd on Tap a letter.

✅ Check: answers "What's this?" with "It's a ___" for 3 or more things.

Next: lessons 5–6 finish Unit 1 (pages 10–13). The phonics page (p. 11) will feel easy because a, b, c, d are already known.

For the children (they can use these alone)

All of these talk and use pictures, so no reading is needed. Sit with them the first time, then 10–15 minutes alone is plenty.

For you

  • Family and Friends 1 Class Book and Workbook, with the Class Book audio. The Workbook often comes with an Online Practice code with games for each unit.
  • Oxford's Family and Friends student site: extra games and activities by level.
  • ESL Kids Lab and Twinkl: printable flashcards and letter tracing sheets.

Good to have in the room

  • A puppet or soft toy that "only speaks English"
  • Real objects for each unit (school things, toys, clothes, plastic food)
  • Play-dough and a small whiteboard for making and writing letters
  • Stickers, a dice, number cards 1–10
